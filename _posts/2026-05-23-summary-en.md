This article explains that Japanese companies' broad diversification stems from lifetime employment and a stakeholder-focused governance model that insulates them from shareholder pressure. This analysis challenges the Western emphasis on corporate focus and shareholder value, highlighting a successful alternative model that prioritizes employee and stakeholder interests, which could inform debates on corporate governance globally. The author argues that Japanese firms, run by employees and largely indifferent to shareholders, diversify to retain lifetime employees whose skills are firm-specific, and this system only works when the company is insulated from outside pressure.

hackernews · d0ks · May 22, 15:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48237163)

**Background**: Historically, Japanese companies adopted lifetime employment (shushin koyo) and enterprise unions, which discouraged layoffs and encouraged firms to expand into new businesses to retain employees. This system was supported by cross-shareholding and patient capital from main banks, reducing shareholder influence. The result was a corporate structure that prioritizes stability and growth over short-term profit.

**Discussion**: Community comments express a range of views: some find the analysis insightful but note the system's downsides like low job market fluidity; others caution against romanticizing Japan's model and point out that Western firms also diversified historically. A common thread is that the 'J-firm' exists to continue existing, indifferent to shareholders.

## [Memory Shortage Driven by AI Demand for HBM Raises Consumer Electronics Prices](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/22/memory-shortage/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Memory manufacturers are reallocating wafer capacity from DDR and LPDDR to HBM due to surging AI demand, reducing production of consumer memory chips and causing price increases, especially for sub-$100 smartphones. This shift marks a structural change in the memory market, where AI workloads now directly compete with consumer electronics for wafer capacity, potentially making cheap smartphones and other devices more expensive globally. Only three major memory manufacturers remain, and they have learned to under-provision capacity. HBM consumes over three times the wafer capacity per gigabyte compared to DDR or LPDDR, and its allocation is expected to rise from 2% to 20% by end of 2026.

rss · Simon Willison · May 22, 22:01

**Background**: Memory chips are produced on silicon wafers, with fixed processing capacity at fabs. DDR and LPDDR are common in desktops, servers, and mobile devices, while HBM is a high-speed 3D-stacked memory used primarily in AI GPUs. The limited wafer capacity forces trade-offs in production allocation.

## [Zhipu Launches GLM-5.1-HighSpeed with 400 tokens/s Output](https://docs.bigmodel.cn/cn/guide/models/text/glm-5.1-highspeed) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Zhipu AI has released GLM-5.1-HighSpeed, a high-speed variant of its GLM-5.1 model, achieving an output speed of 400 tokens per second. The model is currently available to select enterprise customers through the BigModel platform. This 400 tokens/s speed significantly reduces latency for real-time AI applications such as coding agents, voice assistants, and live customer service, making GLM-5.1 more practical for production environments. It also highlights the growing competition among Chinese AI firms to deliver high-speed inference capabilities. The model supports function calling, structured JSON output, and the Model Context Protocol (MCP) for tool integration. It is designed for low-latency applications including coding agents, multi-turn code generation, real-time UI building, operational Q&A, voice assistants, and live customer service.

**Background**: GLM-5.1 is the latest flagship large language model from Zhipu AI (rebranded as Z.ai outside China), released under the MIT open-source license in July 2025. It is designed for agentic tasks with strong coding capabilities. The Model Context Protocol (MCP) is an open standard introduced by Anthropic in 2024 for connecting AI models to external tools and data sources. Output speed, measured in tokens per second, is a critical metric for real-time applications.
