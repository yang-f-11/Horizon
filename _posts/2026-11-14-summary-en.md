## [DeepSeek Opens Full Application API Suite with Search, Files, OCR, ASR and TTS](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/78KBHc4-4N5b2vfL9SkutQ) ⭐️ 8.0/10

DeepSeek has opened its full application-tier API suite, letting developers enable parameters in the existing API to gain deep-thinking search, file input, link reading, and voice conversation capabilities, with the server handling tool calling and context assembly automatically. The V4.1-Flash model is already supported across three endpoints, and Search, Fetch, OCR, ASR, and TTS can each be called individually. This shifts DeepSeek from a bare model endpoint provider to an application platform, so developers building agents, RAG apps, or multimodal chat products can assemble most of the pipeline through one API instead of stitching together separate vendors. The open-sourced chat frontend further lowers the barrier, since teams can deploy a working, history-importable client locally on day one. The bundle emphasizes server-side orchestration, with automatic tool calling and context stitching removing the need for developers to manually wire search, fetching, or speech modules. The accompanying deepseek-ai/chat-demo is a backend-less static React client that runs entirely in the browser, storing the API key in client-side settings and persisting chat history and local attachments locally.

**Background**: Large language model APIs traditionally return only text generated by the model, so anything involving web search, reading a PDF or link, or converting speech requires the developer to build and maintain a separate tool layer. Tool calling lets the model decide when to invoke such external functions, but the orchestration logic still usually lives in the application. DeepSeek is now moving that orchestration server-side, bundling retrieval, document parsing, OCR, speech recognition (ASR), and speech synthesis (TTS) into the same conversational API as the V4.1-Flash model.

## [OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet with Amex, Mastercard and Visa](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

OpenAI announced ChatGPT Wallet, a payment wallet built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa that lets ChatGPT agents complete purchases without ever touching a user's real card number. It rolls out today in the US to ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and older who hold a US-issued card. This is one of the first mainstream attempts to give AI agents a sanctioned way to spend money, which could unlock agentic commerce — letting assistants buy goods, book services or pay bills on a user's behalf. By relying on network-issued tokens and per-purchase biometric approval, OpenAI and the card networks are trying to show that agent-initiated payments can be safer than handing over card details. Users approve each transaction individually, reviewing the merchant and amount before confirming with Face ID or a fingerprint, while the payment networks issue a token that stands in for the real card number. Access is currently limited to US ChatGPT Pro subscribers with US-issued cards, so availability, card eligibility and currency coverage remain narrow at launch.

telegram · zaihuapd · Nov 13, 15:43

**Background**: Payment tokenization is a well-established technique in which a card network replaces a real card number with a unique substitute token, so a merchant or app never stores the underlying credentials; Apple Pay and Google Pay rely on the same idea. Agentic payments extend this model to software agents that act on a user's behalf, raising new questions about consent, fraud liability and how much autonomy an assistant should have over a person's money. ChatGPT Pro is OpenAI's paid subscription tier, and the wallet is being positioned as a feature of that plan rather than a standalone product.

## [FAA orders nationwide ground stop after telecom outage, lifts it 4.5 hours later](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/faa-orders-nationwide-ground-stop-departing-flights-reasons-safety-rcna609247) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On November 13, 2026, the FAA issued a nationwide ground stop halting all departing flights in the United States after an outage hit the telecommunications systems used for air-traffic control communications, lifting the order roughly 4.5 hours later. The agency posted advisories on its fly.faa.gov site for both November 13 and November 14 as the situation developed. A nationwide ground stop is one of the most disruptive actions the FAA can take, rippling into thousands of flights, crew schedules and connecting passengers for days afterward. The incident also highlights how much of the National Airspace System depends on a single telecommunications backbone, raising questions about redundancy in critical infrastructure. According to reporting cited by commenters, the outage appeared to affect a telecommunications network overseen by L3Harris Technologies that carries the radio frequencies controllers use to talk to pilots, though it was not immediately clear whether radar feeds were also hit. Officials at a White House briefing characterized the failure as new equipment and patches added by the FAA overwhelming the older system, arguing that the patchwork approach needs to be replaced with one complete system.

**Background**: Air-traffic controllers do not talk to pilots over the public internet or ordinary phone lines; they rely on dedicated, highly reliable circuits and radio links that the FAA procures as a managed service, historically under the FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure (FTI) program. A ground stop is a traffic-management order that holds aircraft on the ground at their departure airports rather than letting them take off and hold in the air, and it is normally reserved for severe weather, security events or system outages. The FAA has experienced comparable telecom failures before — notably a 2009 FTI outage examined in a Department of Transportation Inspector General report — which provides a useful reference for how these failures propagate through the system.

## [GPU-Accelerated Airband Scanner Captures Live Victoria Ground Stop](https://orcasairband.com/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A developer (mmaunder) launched orcasairband.com, a live public scanner that monitors all civil aviation VHF airband frequencies around Orcas Island (KORS), built on an Ettus B210 software-defined radio feeding two RTX 4090 GPUs that channelize and demodulate the entire band at once. The site unexpectedly proved its worth by capturing real-time controller and pilot chatter during an unplanned ground stop affecting Victoria and Vancouver in British Columbia. The project shows that consumer GPUs plus commodity SDR hardware can turn a wide swath of spectrum into a continuous, searchable, transcribed monitoring service, a capability once limited to institutional or government receivers. It also illustrates how hobbyist infrastructure can deliver genuine public value during fast-moving aviation disruptions, when official channels lag behind. The system uses a CUDA-based polyphase channelizer to split the whole airband into narrow channels simultaneously, then demodulates each one, detects transmissions and discards idle data before transcribing; the author posted raw, unedited timestamped transcripts such as 2143Z on 126.400 Victoria Ground and 2151Z on 134.800 Vancouver Centre. Because the output is automatic speech transcription rather than human copy, some passages are marked unintelligible.

hackernews · mmaunder · Nov 13, 22:04 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50490593)

**Background**: The airband is the slice of VHF spectrum from roughly 118 to 137 MHz reserved by international agreement, coordinated by ICAO, for civil aviation voice communications; navigation aids such as VOR and ILS sit just below it at 108–118 MHz. A ground stop is an air traffic control order halting departures (and often arrivals) at an airport, typically because of weather, communications failures or congestion. Software-defined radio (SDR) replaces dedicated analog circuitry with digitized signals processed in software, and a polyphase channelizer is a classic DSP technique for splitting one wideband capture into many narrow channels at once, a workload that maps well onto GPU parallelism.

## [Tim Peters, Early Python Contributor Behind Timsort and the Zen of Python, Dies at 74](https://x.com/Itsfoss/status/2120957452937998716) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Python creator Guido van Rossum announced that Tim Peters, one of Python's earliest and most influential contributors, has died at the age of 74. Peters was Python's second real user, the author of the Timsort sorting algorithm, the writer of PEP 20 (The Zen of Python), and served 13 years on the Python Software Foundation board. Peters' work is embedded in the daily experience of nearly every Python developer: Timsort remains the default sort in both Python and Java, and the Zen of Python still shapes what the community considers "Pythonic" code. His passing marks the loss of a foundational figure in one of the world's largest open-source ecosystems, and his 13 years of PSF board service underscore how much of Python's governance he helped build. Timsort is a hybrid, stable algorithm derived from merge sort and insertion sort that Peters implemented for Python in 2002; it detects naturally ordered "runs" in real-world data and merges them efficiently, and was later adopted by Java 7 for object sorting. PEP 20 distills Python's design philosophy into 20 aphorisms, of which only 19 were ever written down—a fittingly wry detail from Peters himself.

telegram · zaihuapd · Nov 13, 09:55

**Background**: Python is a general-purpose programming language created by Guido van Rossum in the late 1980s, now one of the most widely used languages in the world for web development, data science, and automation. A PEP (Python Enhancement Proposal) is the formal document format used to propose and document changes to the language; PEP 20 is unusual in that it is a philosophical statement rather than a technical specification. The Python Software Foundation is the non-profit that holds Python's intellectual property, funds community infrastructure such as PyPI, and organizes the annual PyCon conference. Timsort's name comes directly from its author, and it has remained Python's default sorting implementation for over two decades.

## [Warning: Telegram Widgets may allow sandbox escape and RCE](https://t.me/zaihuapd/45114) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A Chinese-language Telegram channel (再花频道, @zaihuapd) posted a security warning claiming that Telegram Widgets — the feature introduced in Bot API 10.4 on November 9 that lets bots embed HTML, CSS and JavaScript inside chat messages — has a flawed client-side sandbox that could allow a sandbox escape and remote code execution. The post urges users not to click on bots of unknown origin and to hold off upgrading to clients that support widgets until the issue is clarified. Widgets let bot-supplied JavaScript run inside message bubbles for Telegram's enormous user base, so a sandbox escape would turn an ordinary chat message into a code-execution vector on desktop and mobile clients. If confirmed, the attack surface is exceptionally broad because it only requires a user to open or interact with a message from a malicious bot, not to install anything. The warning is currently unverified: it provides no CVE identifier, no affected client version numbers, and no reproduction details, and Telegram has not published an advisory. The post also dates the Bot API release to November 9, 2026, which appears to be a typo; per Telegram's changelog and coverage, Bot API 10.4 shipped on November 9 with widgets plus TON Connect wallet signing support.

telegram · zaihuapd · Nov 13, 16:17

**Background**: Telegram Widgets are part of Bot API 10.4, released on November 9, and let bots send interactive cards built with HTML, CSS and JavaScript that render directly inside a message bubble, so users can use mini-apps without leaving the chat. Because that content is untrusted third-party code, Telegram clients must execute it inside a sandbox — typically an iframe or embedded webview — with restricted access to the host app and operating system. A 'sandbox escape' means the embedded code finds a way out of those restrictions and runs with the privileges of the client itself, which is what turns a rendering bug into remote code execution. This is distinct from Telegram's older web widgets, which are simple embeddable buttons and post viewers for websites.

## [SpaceX to attempt first full recovery of Starship and Super Heavy next month](https://t.me/zaihuapd/45117) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Speaking on an earnings call, Elon Musk said SpaceX will next month attempt, for the first time, to recover both the Starship upper stage and the Super Heavy booster on a single flight. He acknowledged the first attempt is not guaranteed to succeed but said the odds are higher for the following flight. If it works, it would be the first time a fully orbital launch vehicle has been recovered end to end, which is the core of SpaceX's pitch that Starship can be rapidly reflown at low cost. That reusability is central to NASA's Artemis lunar lander plans and to SpaceX's ambitions for Starlink deployment and point-to-point transport. The previous launch last month was cut short to roughly three hours after one engine shut down early, and neither the booster nor the ship was recovered. Musk's framing suggests the upcoming attempt is a stretch goal rather than a settled plan, with the company treating the following flight as the more likely success.

telegram · zaihuapd · Nov 14, 03:03

**Background**: A reusable launch vehicle is one whose parts can be recovered after flight and flown again, instead of being discarded in the ocean or burned up on reentry; rocket stages are the most commonly targeted components for reuse. SpaceX already lands Falcon 9 boosters propulsively, but Starship adds the harder step of bringing the much larger upper stage back intact. Super Heavy is the first-stage booster that lifts Starship off the pad, and the plan is to catch both vehicles rather than splash them down.
