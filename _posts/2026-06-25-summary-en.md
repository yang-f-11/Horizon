## [Generative AI Use Linked to Lower Exam Scores in Chinese Students](https://cepr.org/publications/dp21577) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A 30-month longitudinal study of 26,811 Chinese secondary school students found that generative AI use improved homework scores by 18% and reduced completion time by 30%, but led to an 18-24% decline in high-stakes exam scores, with full effects appearing after two years. This large-scale study provides counterintuitive evidence that generative AI can harm long-term learning outcomes despite short-term homework benefits, challenging the assumption that AI tools are uniformly beneficial for education. The findings have significant implications for AI policy in schools and for educators worldwide. The study tracked 26,811 students in grades 7-12 over 30 months, focusing on effects in closed-book monthly exams and high-stakes tests like Zhongkao and Gaokao. The negative impact was most pronounced in social science subjects, followed by STEM and languages, and was stronger among lower grades, high-achieving students, and males. About 80% of AI users exhibited 'homework outsourcing' behavior—very short homework time with high scores—and bore the main losses.

telegram · zaihuapd · Jun 24, 05:15

**Background**: Generative AI models like ChatGPT can produce human-like text, code, and solutions to problems, making them attractive for students seeking homework assistance. However, closed-book exams test genuine understanding and knowledge retention, which may be undermined when students rely on AI without engaging deeply with the material. This study highlights the potential risk of using AI as a crutch rather than a learning aid.

## [NVIDIA 45°C Liquid Cooling Slashes Data Center Water Use](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/liquid-cooling-ai-factories/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

NVIDIA announced at London Climate Week that its upcoming Rubin-generation AI data centers will use 100% liquid cooling with coolant running at 45°C, enabling passive heat rejection and reducing water consumption to near zero. This design cuts annual water usage by millions of gallons, saving operators over $4 million per data center, and opens the possibility of repurposing waste heat for district heating, turning data centers into community energy assets. The 45°C coolant temperature allows data centers to use dry coolers instead of evaporative cooling towers, eliminating the need for water in most climates; however, efficiency still depends on ambient temperature, and in hot climates some water use may remain.

hackernews · nitin_flanker · Jun 24, 14:10 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48660178)

**Background**: Traditional data centers rely on air conditioning or water-based evaporative cooling, consuming huge amounts of water and electricity. Liquid cooling is more efficient but typically uses low-temperature coolants that require energy-intensive chillers. By raising the coolant temperature to 45°C, NVIDIA's design can reject heat passively to the outside air without fans or chillers, dramatically cutting both water and energy use.

## [Nub brings Bun-like DX to Node.js without forking](https://github.com/nubjs/nub) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Colin McDonnell released Nub, a Node.js toolkit that uses a --require preload hook to add transpilation via the oxc transpiler, module resolution hooks, and polyfills for APIs like Worker and Temporal, all on top of stock Node.js. Nub improves Node.js developer experience by offering Bun-like features (fast transpilation, modern API support) without requiring a runtime fork, making it easier for existing Node projects to adopt these capabilities. The transpiler is powered by oxc and packaged as a Node-API add-on; polyfills include Worker and Temporal; the approach is purely additive — code ultimately runs on Node's actual engine and stdlib. The project's creator is also the author of Zod and a former Bun employee.

hackernews · colinmcd · Jun 24, 14:14 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48660267)

**Background**: Bun is a modern JavaScript runtime that bundles a transpiler and many built-in APIs, offering a fast developer experience. Node.js is the standard server-side JavaScript runtime but lacks many of Bun's convenience features. The oxc project provides high-performance JavaScript tools written in Rust. The Temporal API is a modern replacement for JavaScript's Date object with better time zone and calendar support.
