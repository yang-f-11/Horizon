## [Caltech Mathathon: First Hackathon for Research-Level Mathematics](https://mathathonchallenge.com/index.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Caltech undergraduates are organizing the Caltech Mathathon, described as the first hackathon devoted to research-level mathematics. The event focuses on LLM-based problem solving and responsible AI use, with all funding going to judges and participants rather than organizers. This event could help define how AI/LLM tools can be used for serious mathematical discovery, rather than just coding tasks. It also highlights student-led initiatives to bring AI education and recognition to campuses where formal programs may be lacking. According to an organizer's AMA on Hacker News, the team is an independent group of Caltech undergraduates, does not speak for Caltech, and receives no monetary compensation. One commenter noted the event appears to involve waiting for an LLM's output for 40 hours, and the organizers emphasize a commitment to responsible AI use.

**Background**: Hackathons are typically short, intensive events where teams build software or hardware prototypes within a day or two. In recent years, large language models (LLMs) have become powerful tools for generating and exploring mathematical ideas, though their output often requires careful verification. A research-level math hackathon extends the format to open mathematical problems, challenging participants to use LLMs in ways that are both creative and responsible. The event is organized independently by students and is not an official Caltech function.

**Discussion**: Discussion on Hacker News was mixed: organizer brian-bfz offered an AMA and clarified their independent status, while some commenters (like Semkas) questioned whether a 40-hour wait for LLM output fits the hackathon format. Others, such as a recent Caltech graduate, praised the effort for addressing the university's weak CS department and giving students a path to learn about AI.

## [Huawei Debuts Kirin 9050 Pro with Logic Folding in Mate XT 2](https://www.news.cn/20260907/adf46c5c003240d28cc3cf6de54f9b5f/c.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Huawei announced the Mate XT 2 trifold smartphone with the new Kirin 9050 Pro chip, marking its first flagship Kirin chip debut in six years. It is also the first high-performance chip to use logic folding technology. The Kirin 9050 Pro signals Huawei's return to high-end chip design despite US export controls. Logic folding could offer a new way to improve transistor density and performance without relying on the most advanced lithography machines. Logic folding arranges logic units in stacked layers within a single chip and adds vertical interconnect channels, which shorten signal paths and reduce latency. Reports suggest the approach could increase transistor density by up to 53 percent for AI computing and reduce the time constant τ to boost performance and power efficiency.

**Background**: Huawei has been cut off from the most advanced chip manufacturing since being added to the US Entity List in 2019. Its previous flagship Kirin chip, the Kirin 9000, powered the Mate 40 series. Logic folding is similar to 3D integrated circuit techniques and through-silicon vias, using vertical layering and interconnection to continue scaling beyond traditional 2D limits.

## [China's Supreme Court Clarifies Liability in AI Disputes, Covering Deepfakes and Pricing Algorithms](https://www.cnr.cn/news/20260907/t20260907_527806795.shtml) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On September 7, China's Supreme People's Court issued a 24-article judicial interpretation spanning five parts to clarify legal responsibility in AI disputes. It rules that using AI to produce recognizable faces or voices without consent may constitute personality rights infringement, and that algorithmic price discrimination harming consumers should be legally actionable. This provides much-needed regulatory clarity for AI applications in China, affecting developers, platforms, and users of deepfake tools, e-commerce algorithms, and automated endorsement systems. It gives courts and businesses a clearer legal baseline, and signals stricter accountability for AI-related harms in the world's largest internet market. The interpretation also addresses unauthorized AI impersonation for endorsements, permitting punitive damages claims, and regulates AI-enabled 'doxxing' and 'human flesh search' that violate privacy rights. Additional covered areas include AI face-swapping, algorithmic pricing, autonomous driving, and intellectual property issues.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 7, 09:32

**Background**: A judicial interpretation in China is an official document issued by the Supreme People's Court to explain how existing laws apply to specific types of cases, and lower courts are expected to follow it. 'AI face-swapping' uses deep learning to replace a person's face in images or videos, creating risks such as fake videos and unauthorized likeness use. 'Algorithmic price discrimination' (often called 'big data killing familiarity') refers to platforms using consumer data and pricing algorithms to charge different users different prices for the same product. 'Kaihe' (opening a box) is Chinese internet slang for doxxing, or publicly exposing someone's private information.

## [Abusive crawlers eat more CPU on git.kernel.org than all legitimate access](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/7/creepy-crawlies/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Konstantin Ryabitsev, a kernel.org maintainer, reports that rendering commit pages for abusive crawlers consumes more CPU on git.kernel.org than all legitimate access combined, including git clones. At any time, 14 CPU cores across five geo-distributed nodes are dedicated to rendering commits as HTML for scrapers. This exposes the serious operational burden that abusive AI crawlers place on web infrastructure, a challenge shared by many content-heavy services. It raises concerns about rising CPU and bandwidth costs for tools like Datasette, which publish many crawlable pages. The 14 cores span five geographically distributed nodes, and legitimate access includes git clones, the standard way developers fetch the kernel. Ryabitsev describes abusive crawlers as part of a 'background radiation' that constantly generates HTML for scrapers.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 7, 23:08

**Background**: git.kernel.org is the official Git hosting service for the Linux kernel, and it uses cgit to render commits and source code as HTML on demand. Abusive crawlers, often automated bots that scrape pages for AI training data, generate far more page requests than human visitors. Each HTML rendering consumes CPU, so aggressive crawler traffic can exceed the load from git clones, which are a more efficient transfer mechanism. Datasette is an open-source tool that publishes data as interactive websites and APIs; its creator, Simon Willison, highlighted this post because Datasette exposes many crawlable pages.
