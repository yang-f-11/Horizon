## [First Complete Reading of Herculaneum Scroll Achieved via AI](https://scrollprize.org/firstscroll) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The Vesuvius Challenge team has successfully read an entire Herculaneum scroll (PHerc. 1667) for the first time using AI and advanced imaging techniques, virtually unwrapping it and reading it end to end. This marks a historic breakthrough in deciphering texts from the only surviving library from antiquity. This achievement opens the door to reading hundreds of other carbonized scrolls from Herculaneum, potentially recovering lost philosophical works by Epicurean and other ancient thinkers. It demonstrates the power of non-invasive digital methods in archaeology and classical scholarship. The scroll is approximately 1.4 meters long and contains about 22 columns of Greek text. The team used CT scanning and machine learning models to detect the carbon-based ink on the carbonized papyrus. Additionally, 140 columns from another scroll (PHerc. Paris. 4) have also been unwrapped recently.

hackernews · verditelabs · Jun 25, 15:48 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48675179)

**Background**: The Herculaneum papyri are over 1,800 scrolls discovered in the 18th century at the Villa of the Papyri, carbonized by the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD. They constitute the only surviving library from antiquity. Traditional attempts to unroll them often caused damage. The Vesuvius Challenge, launched in 2023, offered over $1 million in prizes to encourage non-invasive reading methods using AI and imaging.

## [IBM Unveils World's First Sub-1nm Chip Technology](https://www.reuters.com/business/ibm-unveils-tech-chip-smaller-than-1-nanometer-ai-computing-push-2026-06-25/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

IBM announced the world's first sub-1nm chip technology with a 0.7nm transistor node, using its novel NanoStack 3D stacked architecture. The chip integrates nearly 100 billion transistors on a fingernail-sized die, achieving roughly double the density of its 2nm node from 2021, and can deliver up to 50% better performance or 70% energy efficiency gains. This breakthrough extends Moore's Law into the angstrom era, promising significantly faster and more energy-efficient processors for AI, cloud computing, and mobile devices. However, mass production is likely years away, and the actual physical dimensions are much larger than the node name suggests, a common industry practice. The 0.7nm node, also referred to as 7 angstroms, uses a 3D stacked transistor architecture called NanoStack, which stacks n-type and p-type transistors vertically rather than side-by-side. IBM has licensed its chip technology to Samsung and Japan's Rapidus but has not yet announced a manufacturing partner for this generation.

telegram · zaihuapd · Jun 25, 15:39

**Background**: For years, the semiconductor industry has used node names like '7nm' and '5nm' that no longer correspond to actual transistor dimensions; instead, they indicate generational improvements in density and performance. IBM's new '0.7nm' node follows this convention, promising roughly double the density of its 2nm node. The NanoStack architecture is a form of 3D stacking that places transistors in vertical layers, similar to emerging 3D-stacked FET technologies being explored by Samsung and others, to continue scaling beyond traditional planar limits.

## [Zig's new bitCast semantics and LLVM backend improvements](https://ziglang.org/devlog/2026/#2026-06-25) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Zig devlog published on June 25, 2026 introduces new endian-agnostic semantics for @bitCast and several LLVM backend improvements, which are already implemented in the self-hosted x86_64 backend. This change eliminates endian-dependent behavior in bit casting, making Zig code more portable across targets and simplifying low-level data manipulation, which is crucial for systems programming. The new @bitCast semantics reinterpret the logical bit representation, so casting a [2]u8 to u16 yields the same result on both big- and little-endian targets. Additionally, the LLVM backend improvements enhance code generation quality for Zig programs.

hackernews · kouosi · Jun 25, 14:19 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48673825)

**Background**: @bitCast is a built-in function in Zig that allows reinterpreting the bytes of a value as a different type. Endianness refers to the order of bytes in multi-byte data types; big-endian places the most significant byte first, little-endian places the least significant byte first. The previous semantics caused different results on different targets.

This regulation sets a unified international standard for autonomous driving, reducing fragmentation and enabling smoother global trade and deployment of autonomous vehicles, while ensuring safety across the vehicle lifecycle. The regulation covers product core technical indicators, manufacturer safety management, safety archives, test verification, and post-deployment safety, forming a lifecycle regulatory framework. China contributed technical proposals and test data, and its national mandatory standard aligns with ADS GTR while adding further detail for L3 and L4 levels.

Autonomous driving levels, defined by SAE, range from L0 (no automation) to L5 (full automation); L3 and L4 involve conditional and high automation where the system handles most driving tasks under certain conditions.

## [OS9Map Bridges Mac OS 9 to Modern Web](https://yllan.org/software/OS9Map/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

OS9Map is a new tool that allows Mac OS 9 to connect to modern network services without a proxy, enabling vintage PowerPC Macs to access today's web directly. This tool revitalizes retro computing by removing a major obstacle: the lack of modern secure networking support in Mac OS 9. It allows enthusiasts to use old hardware for online activities without cumbersome proxy setups. OS9Map works by translating between modern protocols (like TLS) and older ones that Mac OS 9 understands. It requires only 16 MB RAM (32 MB recommended), making it suitable for even the most resource-constrained PowerPC Macs.

hackernews · LaSombra · Jun 25, 15:01 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48674484)

**Background**: Modern websites rely on secure protocols like TLS 1.2/1.3, which older operating systems like Mac OS 9 (released in 1999) cannot support. Previously, connecting such vintage machines required a proxy server to handle encryption and network address translation (NAT). OS9Map eliminates the need for an external proxy by performing translation locally on the vintage Mac.
