## [Banned pesticides found in EU imports due to export loophole](https://www.foodwatch.org/en/eu-banned-pesticides-found-in-rice-tea-and-spices) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A report from foodwatch.org found EU-banned pesticides in 14 out of 64 samples of imported rice, tea, and spices, with 12 of those pesticides not approved in the EU. This is attributed to the 'boomerang effect' where EU countries export banned pesticides to third countries, which then use them on crops exported back to the EU. This highlights a significant regulatory loophole that undermines EU food safety standards, as European companies profit from selling banned pesticides abroad while consumers unknowingly ingest residues on imported food. It raises urgent questions about trade policy and public health protection. Of the 64 samples tested, 14 exceeded the maximum residue limit (MRL), and 12 contained pesticides not authorized in the EU. The most problematic products were dried peppers, cumin, rice, and tea. The report notes that modern analytical methods can detect trace amounts far below health concern levels.

hackernews · john-titor · Jun 8, 15:59 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48447062)

**Background**: The European Union has banned numerous pesticide active substances due to their risks to human health and the environment. However, EU regulations do not prohibit the export of these same pesticides to non-EU countries. This allows European manufacturers to continue producing and selling banned pesticides abroad, creating a 'boomerang effect' where residues return to the EU via imported food.

## [China State Security Warns of AI Transit Station Risks](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/KhF9CMZxOzWAKmwbVcTN5A) ⭐️ 7.0/10

China's Ministry of State Security (MSS) has published an official warning about data security risks in 'AI transit station' platforms, highlighting issues like data breaches, model degradation, malware injection, and illegal data cross-border transmission. The Cyberspace Administration of China has launched a nationwide campaign to rectify AI application chaos. This warning signals increased government scrutiny of AI intermediary services, which have proliferated by offering low-cost, convenient access to multiple large language models. Users and businesses relying on these platforms face significant data privacy threats, and the broader AI ecosystem may see tighter regulation. AI transit stations act as third-party APIs aggregating multiple overseas and domestic models, but many lack proper security certifications, encryption, and consent for data usage. Some platforms have been found to resell user data to other model providers for training, and 'model shrinkage' refers to the use of degraded or watered-down model versions instead of the original.

**Background**: AI transit stations are platforms that integrate APIs from multiple large language model providers, allowing users to access services like ChatGPT, Claude, or domestic models with a single key, often at lower prices and with fewer geographic restrictions. They have gained popularity among developers and businesses for cost efficiency and convenience. However, because they are third-party intermediaries, they introduce additional security and compliance risks, especially when handling sensitive or cross-border data.
