## [FDA approves first-in-class targeted therapy for metastatic pancreatic cancer](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-class-targeted-therapy-metastatic-pancreatic-cancer) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved a first-in-class targeted therapy for metastatic pancreatic cancer, a disease driven by KRAS mutations that were long considered undruggable. The approval represents the first RAS-inhibitor indicated for pancreatic cancer and opens the door to broader use across other KRAS-mutant cancers. Pancreatic cancer has one of the lowest survival rates and few targeted treatment options, so this approval addresses a major unmet medical need. Because KRAS mutations occur in a substantial fraction of cancers across many organs, this drug class could eventually benefit patients with lung, colorectal, and other KRAS-mutant tumors. The review was unusually fast: the FDA approved the new drug application just over a month after accepting it, enabled by the agency's CNPV Pilot Program, compared with typical priority-review timelines of 8–12 months. The approval covers metastatic pancreatic cancer specifically, but the class of RAS inhibitors is expected to be expanded to other KRAS-mutant cancers in the future.

**Background**: KRAS is the most frequently mutated oncogene in cancer, and mutations are found in a large share of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, as well as lung and colorectal cancers. For decades, KRAS was considered 'undruggable' because its protein has a smooth surface with few deep binding pockets for traditional small-molecule drugs. Recent structural and chemical biology advances have finally enabled inhibitors to bind to the mutated protein and 'turn off' its growth signal, leading to the new class of targeted therapies exemplified by this approval.

## [Tailcat: A netcat-style tool for Tailscale's encrypted data plane](https://github.com/tailscale/tailcat) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Tailscale has published Tailcat, an open-source CLI tool that works like netcat but routes connections over Tailscale's private, WireGuard-encrypted data plane. It lets devices in the same tailnet exchange data using Tailscale identity and encryption instead of raw TCP/UDP over the public internet. It brings the familiar netcat workflow—file transfer, port testing, and ad-hoc network debugging—into a zero-trust mesh network, so users can connect peers without exposing public IPs or configuring raw tunnels. This signals growing momentum for identity-based, P2P networking utilities built on platforms like Tailscale. Tailcat relies on the tailnet's control plane for authentication and key exchange, while actual traffic flows through the WireGuard-based data plane, meaning connections are encrypted end-to-end and authenticated by Tailscale identity. The repository also supplies a Nix development environment, and commenters have compared it with similar tools such as Iroh and bitbang-cli.

hackernews · nderjung · Aug 26, 17:42 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49452990)

**Background**: Tailscale is a software-defined mesh VPN built on the WireGuard protocol; its control plane handles device registration and key distribution, while the data plane moves encrypted packets directly between devices. Netcat is a classic Unix utility that reads and writes data over TCP or UDP, commonly used for debugging networks, transferring files, and testing ports. Tailcat essentially reimplements this utility for Tailscale's private network, letting users run netcat-style commands between authenticated tailnet devices.
