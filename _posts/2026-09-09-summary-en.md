## [Buckmaster Alleges OpenAI Pressured Him Over Navier-Stokes Priority](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf) ⭐️ 9.0/10

In a newly posted PDF statement, Tristan Buckmaster says he and Levent Alpöge obtained new finite-time blow-up results for the 3D incompressible Euler and related equations, and he accuses OpenAI of trying to co-opt and pressure them around a closely related Navier-Stokes breakthrough. The statement frames OpenAI's announcement as a priority dispute in which his and Alpöge's work was at risk of being used without proper recognition. The underlying 3D Navier-Stokes regularity question is one of the Clay Mathematics Institute's Millennium Prize Problems, so even a related blow-up result or a credible near-miss is mathematically significant. The dispute over priority, user data, and pressure from a frontier AI company also highlights growing tensions between AI labs and independent researchers over credit and disclosure. According to commenters, Buckmaster and Alpöge's August 15 work proves finite-time blowup with smooth forcing for incompressible porous media, the Boussinesq system, and 3D incompressible Euler, but it does not solve the full $1 million Millennium Navier-Stokes problem. Alpöge works at Anthropic, an OpenAI rival, while OpenAI's separate September 8 claim of a 3D Navier-Stokes breakdown, formalized in Lean, has not yet been independently verified.

hackernews · procedurecall · Sep 8, 05:42 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49605915)

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes equations describe how viscous fluids move, and whether their 3D solutions always remain smooth is the open Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven $1 million Clay Millennium Prize Problems. Finite-time blowup means a solution that starts with perfectly smooth data forms an infinite spike, or singularity, after finite time; proving or disproving that possibility is central to the problem. The incompressible Euler equations, the Boussinesq system, and porous-media equations are closely related fluid models that mathematicians often attack first because they are simpler or show similar blowup behavior. OpenAI's announcement claimed a solution-breakdown result for 3D Navier-Stokes generated by internal frontier models and formalized in the Lean proof assistant.

## [Google DeepMind releases AlphaGenome Atlas, a predictive map of human DNA variants](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/alphagenome-atlas/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Google DeepMind has released the AlphaGenome Atlas, a public catalogue that predicts molecular effects and AVI scores for about 9 billion single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) across the human genome. The atlas is produced by AlphaGenome, a deep-learning model that processes long DNA sequences at single-nucleotide resolution. This is a major milestone for AI-driven genomics, giving researchers an unprecedented way to interpret variants in both protein-coding and non-coding regions of the human genome. It is expected to accelerate genetics research, improve the diagnosis of rare and complex diseases, and enhance the interpretation of consumer DNA tests. AlphaGenome takes up to 1 megabase (Mb) of DNA as input and avoids the trade-off between input length and prediction resolution seen in earlier predictors. The resource covers non-coding regions, which make up about 98% of the human genome and are central to gene regulation, and is available via an online portal.

hackernews · utiiiD · Sep 8, 14:55 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49611251)

**Background**: The human genome is a sequence of nucleotide letters, and a change in a single letter is called a single-nucleotide variant (SNV). Most SNVs are harmless, but some disrupt gene function or regulation and are implicated in disease. Variant effect predictors are computational tools that estimate the consequences of genetic changes, but many only cover protein-coding parts of the genome or are limited in sequence context. AlphaGenome and the AlphaGenome Atlas extend this idea to all possible SNVs across the whole genome, including regulatory regions.

## [Qwen3.8 27B Quantization Benchmarked: 4-Bit Holds Up, 1-Bit Collapses](https://quesma.com/blog/qwen38-27b-quantizations-benchmarked/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A benchmark of Qwen3.8 27B quantizations reports that 4-bit quantized models largely preserve benchmark quality, while 1-bit quantized variants collapse in performance. The results point to a sharp quality breakpoint at extremely low bit widths. Because 4-bit quantization cuts memory usage significantly with minimal quality loss, LLM practitioners can run Qwen3.8 27B on commodity GPUs. The unusable 1-bit results highlight the practical limits of aggressive LLM compression. According to a commenter's summary, the benchmark showed little score difference down to 4-bit, with only 2-bit scoring somewhat lower before 1-bit collapsed. The analysis also criticized the use of Wilson 95% confidence intervals as an inappropriate measure for run-to-run variation, and noted the missing Q3 tier that is common on sub-16GB GPUs.

hackernews · stared · Sep 8, 14:49 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49611128)

**Background**: Quantization compresses a neural network by lowering the precision of its weights and activations, trading a small amount of quality for major reductions in memory and inference cost. Common 4-bit formats such as Q4_K_M make large open-weight models practical on consumer GPUs. KV-cache quantization similarly reduces the memory of stored key and value activations, which often dominate memory when generating long contexts. Qwen is Alibaba's open-weight large language model family, and Qwen3.8 27B is a 27-billion-parameter model in this family.

## [ASML and TSMC Partner to Move High NA EUV to 12-Inch Photomasks](https://www.nrc.nl/nieuws/2026/09/08/asml-gaat-samenwerken-met-taiwanese-chipgigant-tsmc-om-zijn-nieuwste-chipmachines-te-upgraden-a4936073) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On September 7, ASML and TSMC launched an industry collaboration to transition High NA EUV lithography from 6-inch to 12-inch photomasks. The plan targets a 12-inch photomask pilot line by 2031 and use in advanced-node production by 2033, while TSMC intends to deploy High NA EUV for large-scale advanced-node manufacturing starting in 2030. This transition matters because larger 12-inch masks can reduce field stitching and cut the roughly 30% throughput loss associated with High NA EUV, lowering chip manufacturing costs. It affects the economics and scalability of next-generation chipmaking, and the move is backed by major players including TSMC, Samsung, and Intel. High NA EUV raises the numerical aperture from 0.33 in current systems to 0.55, enabling resolution below 10 nanometers. Switching to 12-inch masks requires reworking an entire supply chain built around the decades-old 6-inch square standard, which industry veterans describe as a significant disruption.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 8, 06:55

**Background**: In semiconductor lithography, a photomask is a fused-silica plate carrying the circuit pattern that is projected onto a silicon wafer layer by layer; today's standard format is a 6-inch square plate. High NA EUV is ASML's next-generation extreme ultraviolet lithography, which increases numerical aperture from 0.33 to 0.55 to print finer features by collecting and focusing more light. Since the EUV wavelength cannot realistically be shortened further, raising the numerical aperture is the key path to better resolution. Moving to 12-inch photomasks enlarges the printable field, reducing the need to stitch multiple mask exposures together for large and complex chips.

An e-ink display is a low-power reflective screen that mimics paper, making it an appealing target for such a hack.
