## [AI's Vast Working Memory Gives It Unique Math Advantages](https://davidepiffer.com/p/ai-isnt-outthinking-mathematicians) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new analysis argues that AI systems, equipped with vastly larger working memory than the human brain, possess unique advantages in mathematical problem-solving—even though they may not literally 'outthink' mathematicians. The piece reframes the AI-vs-human debate around memory capacity and persistence, not innate intelligence. This reframing matters because it shifts attention from raw reasoning power to memory and stamina, which are easier to scale in AI and could reshape how models are trained and evaluated for math and other complex reasoning tasks. It also resonates with ongoing discussions about AI-assisted discovery in mathematics, where AI can explore many more paths than a human ever could. The analysis highlights that AI systems never tire or get discouraged, allowing them to 'out-brute-force' human mathematicians by systematically exploring dead ends. It also notes that AI agents can easily record and reuse negative results—something human mathematicians rarely publish—citing recent projects like TheoremDB that aim to exploit this.

**Background**: Working memory is a brain system that provides temporary storage and manipulation of information needed for complex cognitive tasks like language comprehension, learning, and reasoning. The context window of a large language model is the maximum amount of tokenized input the model can consider at once — effectively its working memory. Human working memory is famously limited (often cited as only a handful of items), whereas modern LLM context windows can span tens of thousands to millions of tokens.

## [Unicode Ghost Characters: A Deep Dive into Their Mysterious Origins](https://www.dampfkraft.com/ghost-characters.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

This article by Paul McCann (polm) investigates 'ghost characters' in Unicode—coded characters with no known meaning or origin, often resulting from scanning errors or historical dictionary mistakes. It traces how these characters entered the standard, particularly through CJK encoding, and discusses their philosophical and historical implications. This matters because it exposes the messy human reality behind supposedly rigorous technical standards like Unicode, showing how errors and ambiguity become permanent. It also raises questions about meaning, identity, and how we preserve written language—affecting linguists, typographers, and anyone who works with text encoding. Ghost characters such as 彁 have been shown to result from poor scans of newspaper articles, with Japanese sources documenting this origin. A commenter notes that vast portions of the Kangxi Dictionary—a major source for CJK characters—consist of ghost characters, and that Japanese resistance to Unicode's Aristotelian essentialism pushed the standard beyond the Basic Multilingual Plane.

hackernews · sensanaty · Aug 15, 14:34 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49310926)

**Background**: Unicode aims to assign a unique number to every character in every writing system, but its CJK (Chinese, Japanese, Korean) block is filled with characters from historical dictionaries, many of which are 'ghost characters'—characters that appear in dictionaries but have no known meaning or use. These often arose from misreading or miscopying over centuries of textual transmission. Ghost characters are a side effect of Unicode's effort to be inclusive and exhaustive, sometimes at the cost of accuracy.

## [Anthropic Shares Six Claude Code Cost-Saving Tips; Prompt Caching Saves Up to 90%](http://claude.md/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Anthropic published a blog post outlining six practical cost-saving tips for Claude Code, including using /clear between tasks and /compact before breaks. The post highlights that prompt caching can cut token costs by up to 90% by billing cached input tokens at 0.1x the normal input price. These tips directly address the high cost of daily AI-assisted development, as developers spend about $13 per day on tokens on average. Wider adoption of these practices could make Claude Code significantly more affordable and accessible for individual developers and small teams. The six tips include running /clear between tasks, locking your model and reasoning settings at the start, using @ to reference files instead of typing paths, adding silent flags or delegating verbose commands to sub-agents, running /context in new sessions, and running /compact before stepping away. Anthropic notes that prompt cache typically expires after about one hour, so compaction should happen while the cache is still valid.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 15, 11:14

**Background**: Claude Code is Anthropic's command-line AI coding agent that assists developers directly in terminal sessions. It charges for tokens, with output tokens priced about five times higher than input tokens; prompt caching automatically stores system prompts, tool definitions, and conversation history so that cached reads are billed at 10% of the standard input rate. The /compact slash command summarizes the conversation history to free up context space, and sub-agents are specialized sub-processes that handle specific tasks to keep the main context lean.
