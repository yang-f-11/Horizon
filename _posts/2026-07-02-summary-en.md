## [First synthetic cell built from scratch grows and divides](https://www.quantamagazine.org/for-the-first-time-a-cell-built-from-scratch-grows-and-divides-20260701/) ⭐️ 10.0/10

Researchers led by Kate Adamala at the University of Minnesota created SpudCell, the first synthetic cell built entirely from non-living chemical components that can grow, replicate its genome, and divide. The work was published online in July 2026. This breakthrough demonstrates that a minimal synthetic cell can exhibit a complete life cycle, moving synthetic biology closer to practical applications such as drug manufacturing, environmental sensing, and understanding the origin of life. SpudCell sidesteps the complexity of a natural cytoskeleton by using a simple physical mechanism for division: it grows by fusing with fatty acid droplets, and division occurs spontaneously due to osmotic pressure. The cell's genome includes synthetic genes for replication, transcription, and translation, but ribosome production is outsourced to an external extract.

hackernews · defrost · Jul 1, 14:20 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48747304)

**Background**: Synthetic biology aims to build living cells from scratch to understand life's principles and create useful biotechnologies. Previous efforts had achieved synthetic genomes and cell growth, but cell division remained a major hurdle because natural division relies on a complex cytoskeleton. SpudCell overcomes this by engineering a simpler division mechanism, marking the first time a synthetic cell has completed a full growth and division cycle.

## [FFmpeg 9.1 introduces improved AAC encoder with limitations](https://hydrogenaudio.org/index.php/topic,129691.0.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

FFmpeg 9.1 includes a new native AAC encoder that offers significantly better audio quality than the previous encoder, but it only supports constant bitrate (CBR) mode and is optimized primarily for 48 kHz sampling rate. This update addresses long-standing quality issues in FFmpeg's AAC encoding, making it a more viable option for archiving and streaming without relying on external encoders. However, the CBR-only and 48kHz-centric design may limit its adoption for general-purpose use, especially for music at 44.1 kHz. The new encoder is limited to constant bitrate (CBR) mode and does not support variable bitrate (VBR) or quality-based encoding. It is primarily optimized for 48 kHz sample rate; other rates like 44.1 kHz or 96 kHz will work but may not achieve the best quality.

hackernews · ledoge · Jul 1, 14:10 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48747116)

**Background**: Constant bitrate (CBR) encoding maintains a fixed bitrate throughout the audio stream, which is simpler but less efficient than variable bitrate (VBR). VBR allocates more bits to complex passages and fewer to simple ones, usually yielding better quality at the same average bitrate. 48 kHz has become a common sample rate in video production and streaming, while 44.1 kHz remains standard for CD audio.
