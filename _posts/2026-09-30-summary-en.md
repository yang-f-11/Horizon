## [Hacker News Debates Whether Anthropic's Opus 5.5 Has Been Nerfed](https://github.com/ninjahawk/livenerf) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A Hacker News discussion around the livenerf project drew 305 upvotes and 134 comments debating whether Anthropic's Opus 5.5 model has been silently degraded since launch. Commenters pointed to dedicated benchmark trackers such as Nerf Bench (bridgebench.ai/nerf-bench), which re-tests models against a launch-day baseline, along with firsthand reports of slower or otherwise altered behavior. If hosted models can change behavior without a version bump, developers building products on top of APIs lose reproducibility and cannot trust their own evaluations, which is a core trust issue for the whole commercial LLM ecosystem. The debate also highlights how hard it is for users to distinguish genuine provider-side changes from psychological effects and infrastructure load. Nerf Bench tests a model on launch day and then benchmarks later runs against that baseline, treating a deviation above 10% as a meaningful change; it reportedly detected a real degradation of Opus 4.6 that Anthropic later acknowledged in a blog post, and it is currently tracking Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra. For Opus 5.5 there is no official confirmation, and the evidence remains largely anecdotal, confounded by factors such as honeymoon effects, peak-time compute constraints, and changes in client-side tooling or permission prompts.

hackernews · bryan0 · Sep 29, 22:36 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49901736)

**Background**: "Nerfing" comes from game terminology meaning to weaken something after release. Because large language models are served behind APIs, providers can change weights, routing, quantization, system prompts, or safety filters without changing the version number, so users often cannot tell whether behavior shifted. Community benchmarks and trackers like the one discussed here exist to detect such silent changes by repeatedly re-running the same standardized tests against an initial baseline. The livenerf project appears to be a community effort to track exactly these claims of model degradation over time.

**Discussion**: Opinion was split: one commenter pointed to Nerf Bench as evidence that degradation is measurable and noted it already caught a genuine Opus 4.6 regression, while another argued "nerfing" is not real in the vast majority of reported cases and that users mostly perceive it because new models fail once task complexity exceeds a threshold (a honeymoon effect). Others offered anecdotes — slower Claude Code sessions with more frequent permission prompts after the Sonnet 5.5 announcement, speculation that Anthropic is strained for compute at peak times, and one user saying Opus 5.5 has actually been surprisingly good.

Working public jailbreaks for current-generation consoles are scarce and high-impact, since they open the door to homebrew, save-game backups, and other uses Sony explicitly restricts. The release also puts pressure on Sony's DRM model and may force the company to harden or disable parts of its browser stack, as it has done in past exploit cycles. Commenters note that any jailbreak also usually depends on additional unpublished bugs (for example in the bootloader) to escalate from code execution in the browser to full control of the console, so the practical usability may hinge on unreleased components and specific firmware versions.

Consoles are locked down with signature verification and DRM so that only code approved by the manufacturer can run, and jailbreaks work by chaining vulnerabilities to bypass those checks and execute arbitrary code. Gaining that control matters to users partly because the PS5, unlike the PS1 through PS4, does not allow copying game saves to a USB drive, instead pushing players toward the paid PS Plus cloud backup service.

## [Delhi Cut Electricity Losses From 50% to 5% by Curbing Theft](https://spectrum.ieee.org/delhi-electricity-loss) ⭐️ 7.0/10

An IEEE Spectrum article, discussed on Hacker News, describes how Delhi reduced its electricity losses from roughly 50% down to about 5%, largely by attacking theft and improving the distribution network rather than by adding generation. The improvement is notable because it was achieved in a fast-growing megacity where unmetered hookups and chronic outages were once routine. Distribution losses are one of the largest hidden costs in developing-world power systems, and Delhi's experience suggests that a large share of the problem is commercial and regulatory rather than purely technical. If the model is replicable, other fast-growing cities could free up enormous amounts of electricity without building a single new power plant, which matters for both ratepayers and climate goals. The core of the fix appears to be metering, anti-theft measures such as insulating overhead lines, and better distribution management, with the elimination of unplanned "load shedding" described as arguably the more revolutionary outcome. Commenters point out a ripple effect: insulated lines also became safe routes for monkeys to travel between neighborhoods and reach upper floors of apartment buildings.

hackernews · rbanffy · Sep 29, 12:43 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49892245)

**Background**: Electricity "loss" in a distribution utility has two parts: technical losses, where energy dissipates as heat in wires and transformers, and commercial losses, where electricity is stolen through illegal hookups, meters are bypassed, or bills simply go unpaid. In many Indian cities these combined losses once approached half of all power purchased, meaning utilities effectively paid for electricity that was never billed. Delhi's distribution business was restructured and privatized in 2002, splitting the city among private licensees such as BSES and Tata Power Delhi Distribution, which is the context in which these loss-reduction efforts took place.

**Discussion**: Commenters largely welcomed the improvement while emphasizing that ending frequent, unplanned outages and the voltage surges that followed them was the change residents felt most. One commenter recalled a 2003 movie-theater blackout in Delhi, another described rushing to unplug expensive appliances when power returned, and a third noted the unintended consequence of insulated lines becoming highways for monkeys. A fourth argued India should build on its abundant sunlight with plug-in standards, rooftop and vertical solar, and self-sufficient gated communities.

## [Guide: Using Any C++ Library in Godot via Conan and GDExtension](https://blog.conan.io/cpp/conan/gamedev/godot/cmake/2026/09/29/Using-Any-Cpp-Library-In-Godot.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A Conan blog post published on September 29, 2026 walks through integrating arbitrary C++ libraries into the Godot engine by combining the Conan package manager, CMake as the build system, and Godot's GDExtension native plugin interface. Rather than requiring engine source rebuilds, the tutorial shows a workflow for pulling in third-party C++ dependencies and exposing them to a Godot project as a native extension. It gives Godot developers a practical path to reuse the enormous existing C++ ecosystem instead of rewriting functionality in GDScript, which matters most for teams that hit GDScript's performance ceiling in simulation-heavy or data-intensive projects. It also strengthens Godot's position against engines like Unity and Unreal for projects that depend on existing native libraries. The article focuses on functional integration rather than profiling, so developers are still expected to measure where GDScript or C# actually bottlenecks before moving code to C++. Commenters flag a concrete pitfall on Linux: you either need a linker versioning script to hide your libstdc++ implementation from the dynamic linker, or you must build against the same older distribution Godot itself was built with to avoid symbol conflicts.

hackernews · czoido · Sep 29, 08:40 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49890051)

**Background**: Godot is an open-source game engine whose default scripting language, GDScript, is convenient but slower than compiled languages. GDExtension is Godot's stable C API for native extensions, allowing compiled code to plug into the engine without recompiling against Godot's source and staying compatible across engine versions. Conan is a package manager for C and C++ libraries, and CMake is the build-system generator commonly used to produce those native extensions; the guide chains these tools together so any Conan-packaged C++ library can be called from a Godot project.

## [Anthropic: GLM-5.3 and Claude Mythos Preview achieve first binary exploitation hijacks](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/29/anthropic-frontier-red-team/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Anthropic's Frontier Red Team evaluated several models on 100 randomly selected tasks from its internal Binary Exploitation benchmark and found that GLM-5.3 achieved full control flow hijacks in 4% of trials, while Claude Mythos Preview did so in 6%. According to the accompanying summary, GLM-5.3 also succeeded in 50 of 410 attempts on ExploitBench, close to Claude Mythos Preview's 56 successes. This marks a capability threshold being crossed: earlier models such as Claude Opus 4.6 and GLM-5.2 failed to succeed on any of these tasks, so a score of zero has now become nonzero. It signals a meaningful escalation in the autonomous cyber-offense potential of frontier and open-weight models, with direct implications for AI safety researchers, security teams, and policymakers weighing model release and guardrail practices. The evaluation covers 100 randomly selected tasks, and GLM-5.3 remains below Claude Mythos Preview, so the headline is a threshold crossing rather than parity. Anthropic also reports that GLM-5.3's safety safeguards can be bypassed with simple methods, with simulated tests succeeding 64% to 100% of the time, and that open weights let users modify the model to weaken its refusals.

**Background**: In binary exploitation, a "control flow hijack" means an attacker takes over a program's execution path — for example by overwriting a return address or function pointer — so that the machine runs code of the attacker's choosing; it is one of the hardest steps in turning a memory-safety bug into real-world compromise.
