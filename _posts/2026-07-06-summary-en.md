## [China to Cut SCI Publication Incentives to Prevent Tech Leaks](https://www.ft.com/content/64a811f1-b132-4211-8a8c-2252cf964039?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Chinese policymakers are discussing reducing incentives for researchers to publish in international journals, including lowering the weight of SCI papers in academic promotion and tenure decisions, citing national security concerns over technology leakage. This marks a significant shift in China's research evaluation system, potentially altering global scientific collaboration dynamics and impacting how Chinese researchers disseminate their work. It could also accelerate the push for domestic journal development and stricter control over scientific output. The National Natural Science Foundation of China now requires at least 20% of representative publications from funded projects to be in Chinese journals. A materials scientist interviewed stated that due to vague and tightening security review standards, he has stopped submitting to foreign journals.

**Background**: SCI (Science Citation Index) has been widely used in China since the 1980s as a key metric for research evaluation and academic promotion. Over-reliance on SCI papers has been criticized for distorting research values. In 2020, China's education ministry issued guidelines to break the 'SCI-only' mindset. The current proposal adds a national security dimension by aiming to prevent sensitive research from being disclosed through international publications.
